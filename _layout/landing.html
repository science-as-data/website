OpenAlex

OpenAlex data for research

OpenAlex provides metadata on scholarly works, authors, institutions, and their relationships. The snapshot stores these records as nested JSON, which requires restructuring for relational analysis.

The pipeline converts the snapshot into linked PostgreSQL tables, preserving source identifiers and relationships. SQL queries support sample selection, aggregation, and export by publication year, topic, author, or institution.

Loading instructions, schema documentation, and example queries support reproducible analysis. A separate publication-date audit assesses the evidence underlying recorded dates.

This query counts work records classified as articles for each publication year from 2015 to 2025. The output contains one row per observed year and its record count.

research_question.sql

How many article records occur in each publication year?

SELECT publication_year,
       COUNT(*) AS works
FROM openalex.works
WHERE publication_year BETWEEN 2015 AND 2025
  AND type = 'article'
GROUP BY publication_year
ORDER BY publication_year;
Illustrative SQL; no result is computed here. Counts depend on the loaded snapshot.

Publication-date assessment

Recorded publication dates may refer to online release, journal issue dates, or other events. The audit compares OpenAlex dates with PDF and source evidence to assess their suitability for temporal citation analysis.

A completed 14 September 2026 report reviews 800 archived PDFs, 100 per decade from 1950–1959 through 2020–2026, drawn from DOI-bearing works labeled English articles. It separates online publication, issue/volume evidence, partial dates, and unresolved events. The findings do not validate every OpenAlex date as the day of first public availability.

For nine sampled works, the audit retrieved linked arXiv submission histories and recorded journal DOI agreement. This is bounded evidence gathering for the date audit; a general OpenAlex–arXiv crosswalk remains planned in Data matching.

Audit design and tooling · 800-PDF results and limitations

Define the unit of analysis

Specify inclusion criteria, assess missing metadata, and record the snapshot release. Joins can produce multiple rows per work; use distinct work identifiers when estimating publication counts.
